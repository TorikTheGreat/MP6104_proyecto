\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a system that hides song metadata in audio files by echo
hiding. Each window carries one bit as an echo
applied by convolution with overlap-add. The decoder classifies windows with the
autocorrelation of the cepstrum and corrects bit errors by a majority vote across
the repeated copies. The
defaults (\SI{750}{\micro\second} and \SI{1500}{\micro\second} delays,
\SI{30}{\percent} attenuation, \SI{26.73}{bit\per\second}) recover
\SIrange{95.7}{98.3}{\percent} of the bits in \SI{20}{\second} excerpts of three
recordings, with wideband
perceptual evaluation of speech quality (PESQ) scores of 3.80 to 4.15. Quality falls with echo amplitude and trades off
against recovery. Voting raises the fields recovered at a \SI{5}{\percent} bit
error rate from 0.15 to 5.15 of seven on average and recovers all three encoded
fields in \SI{60}{\second} of real music, but needs at least three copies and does not
correct dropped or inserted bits. Unencoded files produced no false metadata.
% Texto anterior (Marcelo): (lo cambié para incluir lo nuevo)
% This paper presents an audio steganography system designed to embed and recover metadata using echo hiding. Binary information is encoded by introducing low-amplitude echoes with two different delays into an audio signal. The encoder was implemented in Python and C++17 using convolution and overlap-add, while the decoder identifies the embedded delays through cepstral autocorrelation analysis. Experimental evaluation considered audio quality, bit recovery, and rejection of unencoded signals across four musical genres. The results show that bit recovery depends on echo attenuation, window length, and the spectral characteristics of the audio. Under the evaluated conditions, electronic music achieved complete bit recovery, whereas classical music presented greater decoding difficulties. Although the system successfully demonstrates metadata embedding and extraction under favorable conditions, additional improvements are required to increase robustness across different audio contents.
\end{abstract}
